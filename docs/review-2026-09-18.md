# Fresh-eyes review — Be myself planner

**Date:** 18 September 2026
**Scope:** `index.html` (3,247 lines), `tests/`, `.github/`, `SOURCES.md`, `CHANGELOG.md`, `CONTRIBUTING.md`
**Method:** source reading plus live walkthroughs in Chromium (wizard path, checklist path, return visits, desktop 1280×900 and mobile 375×740). Findings marked *verified* were reproduced in a running browser.

This is a findings report. Nothing in the codebase was changed.

---

## Summary

The tool is in good shape. The content is careful, the regional branching is real rather than decorative, the privacy posture is coherent, and `CONTRIBUTING.md` is one of the better codebase maps I have read — it documents its own sharp edges accurately. 121 Playwright tests plus content-integrity snapshots is a serious safety net for a no-build single-file project.

The problems cluster in two places:

1. **The wizard's sense of progress is broken**, and the checklist has no sense of progress at all. This is the most visible first-time-user issue.
2. **Every question exists twice** — once in the `questions[]` array, once as hand-written checklist markup — with no test covering the copy in either. The two have already drifted. This is the main thing standing between the project and an easy next change.

Almost everything else is small.

## 1. UX and flow

### 1.1 The wizard's progress counter runs backwards — *verified*, size: small

**Observed.** Walking the wizard and answering each question in turn, the counter reads:

```
Question 1 of 20   Are you aged 16 or over?
Question 1 of 19   Do you understand that this is only general guidance?
Question 1 of 18   Where do you live?
Question 2 of 18   Where was your birth registered?
Question 3 of 18   What do you need to update on your documents?
Question 4 of 16   Do you have a visa or eVisa to live in the UK?
```

The user answers three questions and is still on "Question 1". The total drops 20 → 19 → 18 → 16.

**Cause.** In `renderWizard()`, both the numerator and the denominator are computed from questions whose `cond()` passes *right now*:

```js
const visibleQs = questions.filter(q => !q.cond || q.cond());
const total = visibleQs.length;
const idx   = questions.slice(0, step + 1).filter(q => !q.cond || q.cond()).length;
```

`age` has `cond: () => ssg('ageConfirmed') !== 'true'` and `disclaimer` likewise. Answering them writes the flag, so they retroactively erase themselves from both counts. Separately, choosing a goal legitimately prunes questions, which drops the total again.

**Why it matters.** The counter and the progress bar above it are the only "how much is left" signal in the flow. Right now they tell a first-time user that answering questions makes no progress. The goal-driven pruning is genuinely good adaptive behaviour, but it is indistinguishable from the bug when both happen in the first six screens.

**Fix shape.** Count answered questions as answered (track `wizardHistory` length rather than re-evaluating `cond`), and either freeze the denominator after the goal question or drop the total entirely and show "Question 4" with a filling bar.

### 1.2 Bare Escape leaves the site, and unsaved answers go with it — *verified*, size: small

**Observed.** The global `keydown` handler routes any Escape outside an open dialog to `panicExit()`, which hides `<body>` and calls `window.location.replace('https://www.google.co.uk')`. In a browser this navigates away immediately; my automation lost the execution context at the keypress.

Nothing writes `wizardState` to storage until a plan is generated — `sss('wizardState', …)` appears only in `generateWizardPlan()` and `generateChecklistPlan()`. So Escape on question 15 of 16 discards all fifteen answers with no confirmation and no way back.

**Why it matters.** Escape is the universal cancel/dismiss key. Users press it to close a `<details>` panel, back out of a tip, or undo a mis-click — and keyboard and screen-reader users press it more than most. A quick-exit affordance genuinely matters for this audience, but the standard pattern for it is a deliberate one (a dedicated button, or Escape pressed two or three times in quick succession), precisely because single-Escape collides with reflex.

The dedicated ⚠️ Quick exit button already exists and is always visible. The keyboard binding is the risky half.

**Fix shape.** Require a double or triple Escape within ~1s, and/or persist `wizardState` on every answer so an accidental exit is recoverable.

### 1.3 The checklist has no progress indication at all — *verified*, size: small–medium

**Observed.** In checklist view, `#controlBarProgressText` is empty and `#controlBarProgress` is not rendered. The page is **4,967px tall** at desktop width — roughly five and a half screens of radios and checkboxes — with no section navigation, no "you are here", and no indication of how far down the button is.

**Why it matters.** The checklist is pitched as the path for people who already know their situation, but it is the longer and more disorienting of the two. The wizard, which is the shorter path, is the one that gets a progress bar.

**Fix shape.** Reuse the existing progress bar to show scroll-through-sections, or add a sticky section index. The `renumberSections()` machinery for section numbers is already there to build on.

### 1.4 On-screen instructions name a button that is not on screen — *verified*, size: small

**Observed.** Generate a plan in wizard mode → **Edit plan** → **Switch view**. The checklist appears with:

- intro text: *"Choose the answers that apply to you, then click **"Show my action plan"** at the bottom."*
- `#checklistGenerateBtn`: text `Update my action plan`, and `hidden`
- the only visible action: a sticky bar reading `Update my action plan`

The instruction names a button with a different label that is not rendered.

**Cause.** Three separate entry points into the checklist — `startChecklist()`, `openChecklist()`, and the else-branch of `toggleMode()` — each set the generate-button label, its hidden state, the sticky bar and the adaptive note independently. `openChecklist()` also resets `#checklistIntroText`; the other two do not. They have drifted.

**Why it matters.** Small, but it lands at the exact moment a returning user is trying to work out how to change something.

**Fix shape.** One `enterChecklist({ hasPlan })` that sets all five pieces of state; the three callers just pass the flag. See §4.4.

### 1.5 Mobile: chrome eats 36–42% of the viewport, and every control is under 44px — *verified*, size: medium

At 375×740:

| | height |
|---|---|
| hero (`header.hero`) | 194px |
| control bar, start view | 73px |
| control bar, plan view | **115px** (9 buttons wrapping onto **3 rows**) |

That is 267px before the first question and 309px before the first plan step, on a 740px viewport.

All nine plan-view toolbar buttons measure under 44×44 (Guide and Theme are **34×34**). This clears WCAG 2.2 AA 2.5.8 (24×24) but not the 44×44 target that a nine-button bar of adjacent controls really needs — and three of them are destructive or disruptive (New plan, Reset progress, Quick exit) sitting next to ordinary ones.

**Why it matters.** This is a tool people will use on a phone, often in a hurry, sometimes somewhere they would rather not be seen using it. A mis-tap on **New plan** next to **Copy link** is expensive — `restartApp()` clears storage.

**Fix shape.** Collapse the mobile toolbar to 3–4 primary actions plus an overflow menu; shrink the hero after first paint or on scroll.

### 1.6 The progress-tracking affordance is undiscoverable — size: small

**Observed.** Each plan item leads with a `☐` button that cycles through four states. Its only explanation for a sighted user is `title="Click to cycle status"` — a tooltip that does not exist on touch devices and does not appear until hover on desktop. The Usage guide explains it, but you have to know to open it.

Reaching "not needed" requires cycling through "in progress" and "done" (`(current + 1) % 4`), which means passing through a state that triggers the focus-mode hide animation on the way to the one you wanted. The `HIDE_GRACE_MS` grace period was clearly added to soften this — it treats the symptom.

**Why it matters.** Progress tracking is the feature that makes this a *planner* rather than a document. If users do not find it, the plan is a static list.

**Fix shape.** One line of visible copy above Step 1 on first view of a plan, dismissible like the existing tips. Optionally a menu on the status button instead of a blind cycle.

### 1.7 The "community advice" badge is 16 identical tab stops with failing contrast — *verified*, size: small–medium

**Observed.** On a maximal plan there are **16** `.community-note` badges. Each is:

```html
<span class="community-note" role="img" tabindex="0" aria-label="Community advice: this comes from the shared experiences of the transgender and non-binary communities, not from an official source. What happens in your case may differ." title="…same 172 chars…">i</span>
```

- **Contrast 3.93:1** (`#1a73e8` on `#e8f0fe`) at 9.6–10.8px. Needs 4.5:1.
- `role="img"` + `tabindex="0"` is a focusable non-interactive element — it takes a tab stop but does nothing when activated.
- 16 tab stops each announcing the *same* 172-character sentence.
- The text lives only in `title`/`aria-label`, so touch users cannot read it at all.

**Why it matters.** The distinction between community knowledge and official guidance is one of the most valuable things this tool does — and it is currently the least readable element on the page and the most tedious one to tab past. Total focusable elements on a full plan: **124**.

**Fix shape.** Darken the colour, state the meaning once in a plan-level legend, and either drop `tabindex` (leaving the `aria-label` on an inline `<abbr>`-like element) or make it a real disclosure that works on touch.

### 1.8 Duplicate and vague navigation labels — *verified*, size: small

| Where | What |
|---|---|
| Start view | **"What is this?"**, **"How do I use it?"**, and **"? Guide"** all in one bar — the last two open the same `dlgUsage` |
| Footer | About / Privacy / Usage guide / Support / Disclaimer — repeating two of the above again |
| Plan view | **Switch view** is absent; you must click **Edit plan** first to reach it |
| Everywhere | **Switch view ⇄** never says what you are switching *to* |
| Same action, three names | toolbar **Edit plan**, welcome-back **Review my answers**, function `makeChanges()` |

**Why it matters.** The start screen's three help entry points are the first thing a new user sees, and two are the same door. "Switch view" is the app's most interesting affordance and the least legible label in it.

### 1.9 Accessibility notes that did *not* turn up problems

Worth recording, since they are the usual failure points:

- Heading order is clean: `H1 Be myself` → `H2 Your personal action plan` (visually hidden but present) → `H3 Step N: …`. No skipped levels.
- Skip link present and first in tab order.
- Toolbar implements a proper roving-tabindex per the WAI-ARIA toolbar pattern.
- `aria-live` region wired to a genuine `announce()` used throughout.
- Locked options carry `aria-describedby` pointing at real explanation text (`#lock-deed-poll-reason`, `#lock-visa-reason`).
- `prefers-reduced-motion` is respected, including a hard bail-out in `launchConfetti()`.
- No console errors or page errors on any path I walked.

One semantic wrinkle: the status button uses `aria-pressed` for a **four**-state control (`state > 0 ? 'true' : 'false'`). `aria-pressed` is binary/tri-state; "in progress", "done" and "not needed" all collapse to `true`. The `aria-label` carries the real state, so this is a redundancy rather than a loss, but the roles disagree with each other.

## 2. Question order and content

### 2.1 The two views ask the same questions in two different orders

**Wizard** (verified live, in order): `age` · `disclaimer` · `region` · `birthRegion` · `goalParts` · `citizen` (has a visa?) · `deedpoll` · `nhs` · `newGP` · `hmrc` · `driving` · `passport` · `visaUpdated` · `employment` · `dbs` · `dwp` · `services` · `vehicle` · `student` · `birthCertName` · `birthCert` · `grc`

**Checklist** (in order): age gate · disclaimer gate · **§1 About you** (`region`, `birthRegion`, `goal`) · **§2 Long-term goals (additional)** (`birthCertName`) · **§3 Long-term legal goals** (`grc`, `birthCert`) · **§4 Your situation** (`citizen`, `employment`, `dbs`, `dwp`, `student`, `vehicle`, `services`) · **§5 Your current documents** (`deedpoll`, `nhs`, `newGP`, `hmrc`, `driving`, `passport`, `visaUpdated`)

**Observed.** The checklist's grouping is coherent — who you are, what you want long-term, your circumstances, what you currently hold. The wizard interleaves all four categories: it asks about your visa (circumstance) at Q6, your NHS record (current document) at Q8, then back to employment (circumstance) at Q14, then back to birth certificates (long-term) at Q20.

**Why it matters.** The checklist ordering is the better narrative and the wizard is the path recommended to people who "are not sure where to start" — i.e. exactly the people who most need the narrative. Users who switch views mid-flow meet a second, unrelated mental model. And maintainers have to hold both orders in their head.

**Size:** medium. The wizard order is just array order in `questions[]`; the constraint is that `cond()` functions must still see their dependencies answered first.

### 2.2 The most branch-determining question is asked fifth — size: small

`goalParts` ("What do you need to update?") prunes six or more downstream questions — verified: the total drops from 18 to 16 the moment it is answered, and name-only removes `birthCert` and `grc` entirely while gender-only removes `deedpoll`, `services`, `vehicle` and `birthCertName`.

Asking it first (right after the two gates) would shorten every subsequent path, and would stop the denominator collapsing mid-flow (§1.1).

### 2.3 "Where was your birth registered?" is asked of everyone, early, and is inert for most — size: small

**Observed.** Asked at Q4 of every user, with five options. It only affects output in three places:

- `birthCertName` question visibility (Scotland- or NI-registered only)
- `p.bornInNI` → the Irish passport route
- `PLAN_ITEMS.birthcert` regional variant — which only renders if `p.birthCert` is true

`CONTRIBUTING.md` states outright that nothing keys off `birthRegion === REGION.W`, so England and Wales are identical here.

**Why it matters.** For an England- or Wales-born user with a name-only goal — a large share of users — this question changes nothing at all. Asked fourth, before the tool has established what the user is trying to do, it reads as bureaucratic. Questions that visibly change nothing are the ones that make a form feel like a form.

**Fix shape.** Ask it after `goalParts`, and consider gating it on "do you want to change anything on your birth certificate, or were you born in Scotland or Northern Ireland?" — though note the gate itself may be more confusing than the question. At minimum, move it later.

### 2.4 The visa is asked as two questions, far apart, in opposite polarities — size: small–medium

- **Wizard Q6** `citizen`: "Do you have a visa or eVisa to live in the UK?"
- **Wizard Q13** `visaUpdated`: "Does your UK visa or eVisa need updating with your new details?"
- **Checklist §4**: "I have a UK visa or eVisa"
- **Checklist §5**: "UK visa or eVisa — *Tick this once your visa or eVisa is already updated … **This is different from the earlier question about whether you have one.***"

That last clarification is the tool apologising for its own structure.

Worse, the wizard's option values are inverted relative to their labels:

```js
o:() => [[S.NO,'Yes, it needs updating'],[S.YES,'No, it is already updated or does not need updating', locked]]
```

Answering "Yes, it needs updating" stores `'no'`. It is correct — `visaAlreadyUpdated: wizardState.visaUpdated === S.YES` — but the question text and the stored value have opposite polarity, and the checklist phrases the same fact the other way round again.

**Fix shape.** Collapse to one three-state question matching the shape already used for driving licence and passport: *"I do not have one / It has my old details / It is already updated."* That removes a question, removes the clarification note, removes the inversion, and makes the fourth identity-document question consistent with the other three.

### 2.5 The same concept uses four different answer shapes — size: medium

| Question | Shape |
|---|---|
| driving, passport | three-state radio: needs update / updated / none |
| employment | three-state radio: needs update / updated / no |
| NHS, HMRC, deed poll | yes / no |
| visa | boolean pair, inverted (§2.4) |
| GRC | three-state radio: no / plan to / already have |

"Do you have a deed poll?" and "Is your NHS record updated?" are asking structurally different things (possession vs. state) through the identical yes/no control, which is why both need clarifying notes — see §3.4.

### 2.6 The same question is worded differently in each view — *verified*, size: small

Comparing the `<em class="note">` strings between the wizard `questions[]` and the checklist markup: **7 exact duplicates and 3 that have already diverged.**

| | Wizard | Checklist |
|---|---|---|
| eVisa note | *"…the order you do things in matters."* | *"…the order you do things in matters. **In most cases, update your passport before your eVisa.**"* |
| DWP note | *"Universal Credit, **Personal Independence Payment (PIP)**, State Pension…"* | *"Universal Credit, **PIP**, State Pension…"* |
| DWP note (NI) | includes State Pension | omits State Pension |
| student question | "Do you have **educational qualifications or certificates** to update?" | "I have **qualifications, student records, or professional registrations** to update" |

The checklist's eVisa note carries a sentence of real guidance the wizard's does not.

**Why it matters.** Same fact, two sources of truth, no test. The content-integrity snapshot covers `PLAN_ITEMS`/`SERVICES` only — `questions[]` text and checklist markup are exactly the uncovered surface, which is why these three could drift silently. See §4.7.

### 2.7 Gaps in question coverage

Flagged as candidates, not omissions — each is in scope given what the plan already discusses:

- **Marital status.** The plan's GRC evidence checklist asks for *"Marriage or civil partnership certificates, if ever married"*, and the detail text explains that a spouse or civil partner must complete a statutory declaration for the applicant to stay married. **Spousal consent is one of the heaviest parts of the GRC process** and it is currently buried inside a closed `<details>` on one item, reachable only if the user has already said they want a GRC. Nothing asks about it, and there is no plan step for updating a marriage or civil partnership certificate itself.
- **What evidence the user already holds.** The plan repeatedly tells users to supply a deed poll copy to multiple organisations. Nothing asks how many certified copies they have — a practical, cheap question that would let the plan say "you will need roughly N copies."
- **Whether the old name must stay usable.** Several steps (qualifications, professional registers, references) interact with needing the previous name to remain verifiable. The plan hints at this; no question surfaces it.

### 2.8 Questions whose answers are load-bearing — a positive finding

I traced every field on `planParamsFromWizard()` into `buildSharedPlan()`. All of them affect output except `svcNone`, which is UI-only state and correctly so. There are no dead questions. `vehicle` → `PLAN_ITEMS.v5c`, `student` → costs + step, `dbs` → gated on `isEmployed`, `bornInScot` → a single cost line. That last one is thin but real.

## 3. Presentation and content structure

### 3.1 The single long page still works — with one exception — size: n/a (assessment)

Measured on a maximal plan (12 items, all branches on):

| | collapsed | fully expanded |
|---|---|---|
| words | 1,257 | **4,177** |
| `#planContent` height | 5,158px | **13,840px** |
| `<details>` closed / open | 13 / 1 | — |

The progressive disclosure is working: 13 of 14 detail panels ship closed, so the default plan is a scannable 12-row list and the 4,000-word version is opt-in per item. This is the right structure and I would not split the plan into pages.

**The checklist is the exception** — 4,967px of always-visible form with no progress and no navigation (§1.3). It is the one view where the single-page approach has outgrown itself. Candidate: collapse sections 2–5 into `<details>` that open as the user works down, which the existing `renumberSections()` + `data-section-label` machinery already half-supports.

### 3.2 The plan's first screen is meta-content — size: small

Above Step 1 sit two stacked `<details open>` boxes — *"⏳ Estimated time and costs"* and *"💡 Did you know about titles?"* — each with its own separate **"Don't show this again"** button writing its own separate localStorage key (`planSummaryDismissed`, `titlesInfoDismissed`).

The user has just clicked "Show my action plan" and the first thing they get is two dismissible info panels. The titles box in particular is genuinely useful content that has nothing to do with the plan that was just generated for them.

**Fix shape.** Ship the titles box closed, or move it below Step 1. Two independent "don't show again" buttons in the first 400px is also a lot of dismissal UI.

### 3.3 Phase colour carries no meaning, and half the palette is dead — *verified*, size: small

`nextPhaseColour()` returns `'phase-' + (phaseIdx++ % 4)`, so a step's colour is purely its position. Reorder the plan and `renumberPhases()` reassigns colours by new position — so colour is not even a stable identifier for a step.

Meanwhile `--phase-0-bg` through `--phase-3-bg` are **defined in both light and dark themes and referenced nowhere**. The only use of the phase palette is `border-color`, through a cascade chain that works only by source order:

```css
.phase-0{border-color:var(--phase-0)}
.phase-1,.phase-2,.phase-3{border-color:var(--phase-1)}
.phase-2,.phase-3{border-color:var(--phase-2)}
.phase-3{border-color:var(--phase-3)}
```

Four rules to express four independent assignments, each overriding the last.

### 3.4 Three separate notes patch the same ambiguity — size: small

The checklist has to say all of these:

- NHS: *"Tick this once it is already updated with your new details, **not just because you already have one**."*
- HMRC: *"Tick this once you have already updated your HMRC and tax records, **not just because you pay tax**."*
- Visa: *"Tick this once your visa or eVisa is already updated … **This is different from the earlier question about whether you have one**."*

Three clarifications, all fixing the same thing: a bare checkbox does not say whether ticking means *"I have this"* or *"I've done this"*. This is accreted patching of a structural problem. §2.5's three-state radio shape says it in the control instead of in a footnote.

### 3.5 Tone is consistent; the iconography is not — size: small

The writing voice is steady and good — plain, second-person, non-patronising, and it resists both clinical coldness and forced cheer. Two wobbles:

- The all-done state (*"You should be proud of yourself. Take some time to celebrate!"* plus confetti) is warmer than anything else on the page. Not wrong — arguably earned — but it is the only place the voice does that.
- **⚠️ carries three different meanings**: danger (Quick exit), important caution (*"⚠️ Gender service waiting list"*, *"⚠️ Minimum age"*), and privacy warnings (*"⚠️ The new certificate will show both your old and new names"*). Alongside 💡 for tips, 🎉 for success, and the blue `i` badge for community advice, that is four information registers and the most severe symbol is the ambiguous one.

### 3.6 `dlgAbout` and `README.md` are the same document — *verified*, size: small

Five verbatim shared runs of 80+ characters (~950 characters total), under the identical eight headings: *What is this? / Who is it for? / Is my information safe? / Can I use this offline? / How does it work? / Step-by-step or checklist? / Is this legal advice? / How do I save or share my plan?*

Two copies, no test, and the README is the one a contributor edits.

### 3.7 CHANGELOG labelling — size: small

The category scheme (⚠️ Government or policy / Information / Wording / Code / Layout / Infrastructure) is genuinely good and worth keeping. But the current file has **ten** entries titled *"September 2026 Update (Part N)"*. "Part ten" tells a reader nothing; dates or a one-line theme would.

## 4. Code structure and maintainability

The question asked was "what would make the next change easier", so these are ordered by how much they cost the *next* edit.

### 4.1 Every question lives in six to eight places — size: large (but splittable)

To add one question today you must touch:

1. `questions[]` — wizard definition, `cond`, options
2. hand-written checklist markup in `#checklistView`
3. `CHK_MAP` — id ↔ state-key pair (checkbox questions only)
4. `renderChecklist()` — state → DOM
5. the delegated `change` handler on `#checklistView` — DOM → state
6. `updateLocks()` — show/hide wiring
7. often `updateDerivedState()` — cross-question invariants
8. often `planParamsFromWizard()` — expose to the plan builder

…plus `loadUrlParams()` if it belongs in a shared link.

`CONTRIBUTING.md` documents this honestly — *"Anything added to the checklist needs both directions wired up, or you get sync bugs"* — and cites two real past bugs of exactly this kind. The documentation is doing the job the code should be doing.

**Why it matters.** This is the single biggest tax on the next substantial change, and it is what produced §1.4, §2.6 and §3.4. It is also why the wizard and checklist orders diverged (§2.1): there is no shared definition to keep them aligned.

**Fix shape — incremental, not a rewrite.** Extend each `questions[]` entry with what the checklist needs (`section`, `checklistLabel`, control type) and render one checklist section from the array. The first section proves the pattern; the rest follow as convenient. This is the change I would prioritise if the project expects more questions. It does not require a framework, a build step, or breaking the single-file constraint — `renderWizard()` already builds its markup from the same array.

### 4.2 CSS is one 25,998-character line — size: small, high leverage

Line 27 holds the entire stylesheet minified onto a single line. `CONTRIBUTING.md` accepts this and advises *"search for a selector or property rather than a line number"*.

**Why it matters.** The brief names "restyling" as a candidate future change, and this is the main thing that makes restyling painful. You cannot diff it usefully — any CSS change shows as one modified line of 26,000 characters, so code review of a style change is effectively impossible. You cannot comment it. The dead `--phase-*-bg` variables (§3.3) and the source-order-dependent `.phase-N` chain survived precisely because nobody can read the file.

**Why it costs nothing to fix.** There is no build step to change, no tooling to add. Unminified CSS in a single file is ~8KB larger before gzip and near-identical after. The "single file, no build step" principle is untouched.

### 4.3 The `window.X = X` export block — size: medium

Roughly **50** manual export lines at the end of the script, because everything is inside an IIFE while the markup uses inline `onclick="…"` throughout.

`CONTRIBUTING.md` flags the hazard: *"If you add a new function and wire it to an onclick, it needs a line here too, or the browser will report it as undefined."* A miss produces a silent failure that only appears when a user clicks, with no test unless someone wrote one for that specific button.

**Fix shape.** One delegated `click` listener keyed on a `data-action` attribute, dispatching through a handler map. The map is the export list, except that a missing entry is visible in one place instead of inferred from a runtime error. This deletes 50 lines and the footgun with them.

### 4.4 Three checklist entry points duplicate the same state setup — size: small

`startChecklist()`, `openChecklist()` and `toggleMode()`'s else-branch each independently set the generate-button label, its `hidden` class, the sticky bar's visibility and `aria-hidden`, the two warning banners, and the adaptive note. They already disagree about `#checklistIntroText` — which is the §1.4 bug.

`CONTRIBUTING.md` even warns about this class of thing generically: *"Any one-time UI element … needs to be explicitly reset by every 'fresh start' entry point … not just the one place that shows it."*

**Fix shape.** A single `enterChecklist({ hasPlan, fresh })`.

### 4.5 Duplicated derivation and dead locals — *verified*, size: small

`updateDerivedState()` (line ~1501) and `updateLocks()` (line ~1585) open with the same eleven-line block computing `goal`, `region`, `isGender`, `isName`, `isNonUK`, `empVal`, `isEmployed`, `hasDeed`, `isScot`, `isNI`.

Dead locals in that duplicated preamble:

- `updateDerivedState()`: `isName`, `isScot`, `isNI` — declared, never read
- `updateLocks()`: `isScot`, `isNI` — declared, never read

A shared `derive()` returning the object removes the duplication and the dead variables together.

### 4.6 String-keyed coupling across unrelated code — size: medium

The same string identifies a thing in several places that do not reference each other:

- `st_trk_<id>` — localStorage progress keys, generated in `planItemHtml()`, read by `restoreTrackers()`, serialised into the share link's `prg` payload
- `svc_detail_<key>` — DOM ids built inside `SERVICES[].detailFn` template strings, looked up by `applySvcDetailState()` via `trackId.replace('trk_svc_','')`
- `data-phase-key` — used for order persistence and the `computePlanFingerprint()` check
- `phase_s<n>` — heading ids reassigned by `renumberPhases()` on every reorder

`CONTRIBUTING.md` correctly warns *"never renumber or reuse a key"* for evidence items, since saved ticks and shared links both depend on them. The risk is that renaming an id is a silent data-loss event for anyone with a saved plan, and nothing but a hand-written test catches it.

One instance reads worse than it is: `id="svc_detail_council"` appears three times in the source, as do `svc_detail_electoral` and `svc_detail_landreg`. They are mutually exclusive ternary branches inside one `detailFn`, so only one reaches the DOM — not a live duplicate-ID bug, but it defeats grep and would become one if a branch were ever made additive.

### 4.7 The content-integrity snapshot has a hole where the drift is — size: small

`content-snapshot-lib.js` captures `PLAN_ITEMS` and `SERVICES` strings, which is the bulk of the guidance and the right priority. It does not capture `questions[]` text or the checklist markup — which is exactly where §2.6's three diverged strings live, and why they diverged silently.

Extending `extractContentMap()` to cover question text and checklist labels would close it. If §4.1 lands, the two collapse into one source and the snapshot covers both for free.

### 4.8 Naming inconsistencies — size: small

- `wizardState` holds checklist state too; `CONTRIBUTING.md` has to explain that it is shared.
- `chkNonUK` ↔ `citizen` — the id says "non-UK", the state key says "citizen", and the question asks whether you hold a visa. Three names, none of which is "hasVisa".
- `chkStudent` ↔ `student` for a question that covers professional registrations (NMC, GMC, legal regulators) as much as students.
- Two region vocabularies: `REGION` uses `e`/`w`/`s`/`ni`; `PLAN_ITEMS.regions` uses `ni`/`scot`/`wales`/`default`. `CONTRIBUTING.md` documents this as deliberate, and the reasoning is sound, but it is still two mappings to hold.

### 4.9 `CONTRIBUTING.md`'s line map is stale — size: small

It states *"HTML body (lines ~382 to 806)"* and *"JavaScript (lines ~806 to 3025)"*. Actual: body ~30–490, script 492–3245. The file does hedge (*"exact line numbers shift … use them as a starting point"*), but these are off by 350+ lines and point into the wrong section entirely — the "body" range lands inside the JavaScript.

Given that this is the only structural map of a 3,247-line file, it is worth either correcting or replacing with section markers (`/* ===== SECTION: … ===== */`) that cannot go stale.

### 4.10 What is already good

Worth stating, because it constrains what "improvement" should mean here:

- `PLAN_ITEMS` / `SERVICES` as a content layer separate from rendering is the right shape, and it is where most maintenance actually happens. The `regions` / `variants` / `evidence` resolution helpers (`planItemRegion`, `planItemVariant`, `planItemEvidence`) are small, composable and consistent.
- The four-state tracker is genuinely generic — keyed on a track-id string with `data-svc-parent` for grouping, not hardcoded per feature. Evidence checklists and the services list reuse it unchanged.
- Order persistence validates against a plan fingerprint and resets gracefully with an announcement rather than silently misapplying stale order.
- `buildSharedPlan()` is wrapped in try/catch with a user-facing recovery message that names the likely cause and the fix.
- Legacy share-link support (`REGION_LEGACY`, `handleLegacyUrl`) means old links still work. That is care most projects do not take.
- The CSP meta tag, `'self'`-only, with no external scripts, styles or fonts, is consistent with the privacy claim rather than merely asserted alongside it.
